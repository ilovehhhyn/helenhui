dear shanghai, although my knee is gravely scraped i still love you very very much. i will still bike—-to be fair i was biking at my 21th hour awake after the flight, and i really cannot see much in the dark. shanghai i missed you, the trees and excruciatingly humid afternoon strolls. i felt so alone and new in the vast body of yours, the beautiful feminine body feeding on the youth and elixir of western capital, the steadiness and eternity of silverlined names, the promise of the material. this body that is all of our bodies. i do feel much prettier when i come back to shanghai i don’t know why. “you fall in love too easily,” he said. i didn’t tell him that two weeks ago i wrote “i am falling in love too easily” word for word, although in a different way that he meant it. today he said that he couldn’t believe how similar we were. did i love whoever i dated? one of them yes. the others i wouldn’t call these love but rather bursts of feelings needing an anchor, like a spring bird needing a resting branch and you were a pretty looking flower. do i love my friends? yes. do i love the spinal mountains by the shore of california? yes. how vast must we be to love. how vast must you be. photo is death i read. my love is to take photos. love is pretty. it’s quite simple, your glistening eyes, your smile your laugh, the humming of time and earth as the funeral march, a festival mourning a death at the end of the odyssey. and that’s love, the instant with a gush of history behind the hand pressing the shutter. i remember leaving a post-it note when i first read the Odyssey saying why do people drink wine and hold contests for three days to mourn a hero. death and festivity. every photo i take of you—you walking, you in golden hour, you talking to me over the screen with the blue blue sky behind you, you singing, you in early morning—becomes an urn i want to mourn with wine and trumpet songs, emphasis not on the urn but on the wine and trumpet songs. those are eternal. those are love.
